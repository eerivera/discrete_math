# Skill Description

The ability to give a formal truth-tree proof that a first-order argument is valid and explain each step. In particular, you should be able to:

* negate the conclusion and simplify the premises and negated conclusion;
* Skolemize existential quantifiers with fresh constants or functions;
* instantiate universal formulas with appropriate ground terms; and
* close every branch by identifying a formula and its negation.

Each practice exam problem gives one argument with several premises and asks for a proof of validity. Countermodels and nonterminating searches are supporting ideas in the tutorial, rather than additional tasks in these practice problems. All domains are nonempty.

# Tutorial

Use the [dot notation and scope convention](../../notes/predicate_calculus/overview.md#dot-notation-and-quantifier-scope) throughout the proof. Review [F07](F07.md) for simplification and Skolemization.

To test whether premises $E_1,\ldots,E_n$ logically imply a conclusion $C$:

1. place all premises and $\neg C$ on one tree;
2. rename bound variables as needed, eliminate derived connectives, and move negations inward;
3. Skolemize existential quantifiers, using a different fresh symbol for each existential quantifier across the whole set of premises and the negated conclusion;
4. apply the [propositional truth-tree rules](../Propositional_Calculus/G01.md#truth-tree-operations); and
5. instantiate universally quantified formulas with ground terms as needed.

A **ground term** contains no variables. If the new constants are $a,b$ and the new unary function is $f$, examples include $a$, $b$, $f(a)$, and $f(f(b))$.

If there are no constants after Skolemization, introduce one fresh constant to name an element of the nonempty domain.

Keep the universal formulas available throughout the proof: using one instance does not use up the formula. Apply an instance on each open branch where it is needed. A universal formula may be instantiated more than once. From $\forall x . F(x)$, any ground term $t$ permits the instance $F(t)$. Choose terms that can expose contradictions already suggested by the branch.

A branch closes when it contains an atomic formula and its exact negation, with matching terms. For example, $P(a)$ and $\neg P(b)$ alone do not close a branch. Different constant symbols need not denote different objects, but they are not automatically interchangeable.

## Closure and countermodels

If every branch closes, the premises together with the negated conclusion are unsatisfiable, so the argument is valid. First-order truth trees need not terminate when an argument is invalid: function symbols may generate infinitely many ground terms. A finite open branch can establish invalidity only when it supplies enough information to construct and verify a countermodel.


## Worked example: one premise

Before tackling the multi-premise practice, consider this shorter argument.

**Premise:**

$$
\forall x . \bigl(P(x)\rightarrow\exists y . Q(x,y)\bigr)
$$

**Conclusion:**

$$
\forall x . \bigl((\forall z . \neg Q(x,z))\rightarrow\neg P(x)\bigr)
$$

Simplify and Skolemize the premise:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\forall x . \bigl(P(x)\rightarrow\exists y . Q(x,y)\bigr)
&\equiv \forall x . \bigl(\neg P(x)\vee\exists y . Q(x,y)\bigr)\\
&\leadsto \forall x . \bigl(\neg P(x)\vee Q(x,f(x))\bigr),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $f$ is a fresh Skolem function. The symbol $\leadsto$ marks the satisfiability-preserving Skolemization step.

Negate and simplify the conclusion:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\neg\forall x . \bigl((\forall z . \neg Q(x,z))\rightarrow\neg P(x)\bigr)\\
&\quad\equiv \exists x . \neg\bigl((\forall z . \neg Q(x,z))\rightarrow\neg P(x)\bigr)\\
&\quad\equiv \exists x . \bigl((\forall z . \neg Q(x,z))\wedge P(x)\bigr)\\
&\quad\leadsto (\forall z . \neg Q(a,z))\wedge P(a),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $a$ is a fresh Skolem constant.

The tree starts with $P(a)$ and $\forall z . \neg Q(a,z)$. Instantiate the premise at $x=a$:

$$
\neg P(a)\vee Q(a,f(a)).
$$

The $\neg P(a)$ branch closes against $P(a)$. On the other branch, instantiate $\forall z . \neg Q(a,z)$ at $z=f(a)$ to obtain $\neg Q(a,f(a))$, which closes against $Q(a,f(a))$.

![Closed truth tree for the G02 practice inference](images/G02/practice-truth-tree.svg)

Every branch closes, so the argument is valid.

# Answers

## Practice Exam Problem 1

### Simplify and Skolemize

Eliminate the implication in p1 and replace its existential witness by a fresh function $f(x)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\forall x . \bigl(P(x)\rightarrow\exists y . (R(x,y)\wedge Q(y))\bigr)\\
&\quad\equiv \forall x . \bigl(\neg P(x)\vee\exists y . (R(x,y)\wedge Q(y))\bigr)\\
&\quad\leadsto \forall x . \bigl(\neg P(x)\vee(R(x,f(x))\wedge Q(f(x)))\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

The witness may depend on $x$, so it must not be replaced by a single constant. The arrow $\leadsto$ marks Skolemization, which preserves satisfiability in the expanded language rather than logical equivalence. Premise p2 becomes:

$$
\forall y . (\neg Q(y)\vee S(y)).
$$

Negate the conclusion, eliminate its implication, and move the negations inward:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\neg\forall x . \bigl(P(x)\rightarrow\exists z . (R(x,z)\wedge S(z))\bigr)\\
&\quad\equiv \exists x . \bigl(P(x)\wedge\neg\exists z . (R(x,z)\wedge S(z))\bigr)\\
&\quad\equiv \exists x . \bigl(P(x)\wedge\forall z . (\neg R(x,z)\vee\neg S(z))\bigr)\\
&\quad\leadsto P(a)\wedge\forall z . (\neg R(a,z)\vee\neg S(z)).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $a$ is a fresh constant: the existential quantifier in the negated conclusion is not within a universal quantifier's scope.

### Close the tree

Split the conjunction from the negated conclusion into $P(a)$ and $\forall z . (\neg R(a,z)\vee\neg S(z))$.

1. Instantiate p1 at $x=a$. Its $\neg P(a)$ branch closes against $P(a)$; its other branch supplies $R(a,f(a))$ and $Q(f(a))$.
2. On that branch, instantiate the universal formula from the negated conclusion at $z=f(a)$. The $\neg R(a,f(a))$ branch closes against $R(a,f(a))$.
3. On the remaining branch, $\neg S(f(a))$ holds. Instantiate p2 at $y=f(a)$, obtaining $\neg Q(f(a))\vee S(f(a))$. The two alternatives close against $Q(f(a))$ and $\neg S(f(a))$, respectively.

![Closed truth tree for practice exam problem 1](images/G02/practice-exam-01.svg)

Every branch closes. The premises and negated conclusion are unsatisfiable, so the argument is valid.

## Practice Exam Problem 2

### Simplify and Skolemize

For this separate problem, choose fresh symbols $f$ and $a$. Premise p1 needs a Skolem function because its existential witness may depend on $x$:

$$
\forall x . (R(x,f(x))\wedge Q(f(x))).
$$

Eliminate the implication in p2 and apply De Morgan's law to its negated antecedent:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\forall x . \forall y . \bigl((P(x)\wedge R(x,y))\rightarrow(S(y)\vee T(y))\bigr)\\
&\quad\equiv \forall x . \forall y . \bigl(\neg(P(x)\wedge R(x,y))\vee S(y)\vee T(y)\bigr)\\
&\quad\equiv \forall x . \forall y . \bigl(\neg P(x)\vee\neg R(x,y)\vee S(y)\vee T(y)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Premise p3 becomes:

$$
\forall y . \bigl(\neg Q(y)\vee(\neg S(y)\wedge\neg T(y))\bigr).
$$

Negate the conclusion, remove double negation, and Skolemize with the fresh constant $a$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\neg\neg\exists x . P(x)
&\equiv \exists x . P(x)\\
&\leadsto P(a).
\end{aligned}
$$

### Close the tree

1. Start with $P(a)$. Instantiate p1 at $x=a$ and split the conjunction, obtaining $R(a,f(a))$ and $Q(f(a))$.
2. Instantiate p3 at $y=f(a)$. The $\neg Q(f(a))$ branch closes. Its other branch supplies both $\neg S(f(a))$ and $\neg T(f(a))$.
3. On that remaining branch, instantiate p2 with $x=a$ and $y=f(a)$. Its four disjuncts contradict $P(a)$, $R(a,f(a))$, $\neg S(f(a))$, and $\neg T(f(a))$, respectively.

![Closed truth tree for practice exam problem 2](images/G02/practice-exam-02.svg)

The four-way split abbreviates repeated applications of the binary OR rule; drawing those binary splits separately is equally correct. Every branch closes, so the argument is valid.
