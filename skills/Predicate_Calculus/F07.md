# Skill Description

The ability to simplify first-order formulas for use with the truth-tree method. In particular, you should be able to:

* rename bound variables without changing meaning;
* eliminate $\rightarrow$, $\leftrightarrow$, and $\oplus$;
* move negations inward until they apply only to atomic formulas;
* move quantifiers when the usual free-variable conditions permit; and
* Skolemize existential quantifiers while distinguishing satisfiability preservation from logical equivalence.

---

# Sample Problem

Simplify the following formula by eliminating the implication and moving all negations inward:

$$
\forall x\bigl((\forall y\,P(x,y))\rightarrow\exists z\,Q(x,z)\bigr).
$$

Then Skolemize the result and move its remaining universal quantifiers to the front. Explain which steps produce an equivalent formula and which step produces only an equisatisfiable formula in an expanded language.

---

# Tutorial

## 1. Rename bound variables

Different quantifiers should use different variables. Choose a fresh variable name that does not occur elsewhere in the formula, and rename only the occurrences bound by that quantifier. This avoids capturing a free variable or changing which quantifier binds an occurrence. For example,

$$
\forall x\bigl(P(x)\rightarrow\exists x\,Q(x)\bigr)
\equiv
\forall x\bigl(P(x)\rightarrow\exists y\,Q(y)\bigr).
$$

## 2. Eliminate derived connectives

Useful equivalences include:

$$
A\rightarrow B\equiv\neg A\vee B,
$$

$$
A\leftrightarrow B\equiv(A\wedge B)\vee(\neg A\wedge\neg B),
$$

and

$$
A\oplus B\equiv(A\wedge\neg B)\vee(\neg A\wedge B).
$$

## 3. Move negations inward

In addition to propositional De Morgan laws, use:

$$
\neg\forall x\,F(x)\equiv\exists x\,\neg F(x)
$$

and

$$
\neg\exists x\,F(x)\equiv\forall x\,\neg F(x).
$$

After eliminating derived connectives and moving negations inward, the formula is in **negation normal form**: its only connectives are $\wedge$, $\vee$, and $\neg$, and every negation applies directly to an atomic formula. Quantifiers may remain.

## 4. Move quantifiers carefully

For example, if $x$ is not free in $B$, then

$$
(\exists x\,A(x))\vee B\equiv\exists x(A(x)\vee B).
$$

The side condition matters. Moving a quantifier across a formula containing a free occurrence of the same variable can capture that variable and change the meaning.

## 5. Skolemize

For a sentence in negation normal form, replace each existentially quantified variable with a fresh Skolem symbol. Use a different new symbol for each existential quantifier:

* use a fresh constant when the existential quantifier is not within the scope of a universal quantifier;
* use a fresh function of the universally quantified variables on which the witness may depend otherwise.

For example,

$$
\forall x\,\exists y\,P(x,y)
$$

Skolemizes to

$$
\forall x\,P(x,f(x)),
$$

where $f$ is new.

Skolemization does **not** generally produce a logically equivalent sentence in the same language. It produces a sentence in an expanded language that is satisfiable exactly when the original sentence is satisfiable. This is the property needed by the truth-tree method.

# Answer

Start with

$$
\forall x\bigl((\forall y\,P(x,y))\rightarrow\exists z\,Q(x,z)\bigr).
$$

Eliminate the implication:

$$
\forall x\bigl(\neg\forall y\,P(x,y)\vee\exists z\,Q(x,z)\bigr).
$$

Move the negation through the universal quantifier:

$$
\forall x\bigl((\exists y\,\neg P(x,y))\vee\exists z\,Q(x,z)\bigr).
$$

Because $y$ is not free in $Q(x,z)$ and $z$ is not free in $P(x,y)$, move the existential quantifiers outward:

$$
\forall x\,\exists y\,\exists z\bigl(\neg P(x,y)\vee Q(x,z)\bigr).
$$

Every step so far is a logical equivalence. Now introduce fresh Skolem functions $f$ and $g$. Both witnesses may depend on $x$:

$$
\forall x\bigl(\neg P(x,f(x))\vee Q(x,g(x))\bigr).
$$

The last sentence is equisatisfiable with the original formula in the expanded language containing $f$ and $g$; it is not being claimed as a logical equivalent in the original language.
