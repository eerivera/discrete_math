# Skill Description

The ability to translate between English statements and first-order logic in a specified language and domain. Translations must preserve quantifier order, quantifier scope, and the direction of every predicate. Each practice exam problem follows the exam format: a vocabulary followed by two English-to-logic translations and two logic-to-English translations.

---

# Sample Problems

## Practice Exam Problem 1

Let the domain be a group of people, with:

* $S(x)$: $x$ has COVID;
* $F(x)$: $x$ has a fever;
* $C(x,y)$: $x$ is a close contact of $y$;
* $R(x,y)$: $x$ and $y$ are roommates;
* $V(x)$: $x$ is vaccinated; and
* $I(x)$: $x$ has influenza.

Translate the following into first-order logic.

1. Everyone who has COVID has a vaccinated close contact who does not have influenza.
2. Anyone who has a roommate with COVID and does not have COVID is vaccinated.

Translate the following into clear English.

3. $\exists y . \forall x . C(x,y)$
4. $\forall x . \forall y . \bigl((C(x,y)\wedge\neg V(y))\rightarrow(F(x)\vee I(x))\bigr)$

## Practice Exam Problem 2

Use the same domain and vocabulary as Problem 1. Translate the following into first-order logic.

1. If everyone is vaccinated, then no one has COVID.
2. If someone has COVID, then every unvaccinated close contact of that person has COVID.

Translate the following into clear English.

3. $\exists x . \bigl(V(x)\wedge\forall y . (R(x,y)\rightarrow\neg I(y))\bigr)$
4. $\forall x . \bigl((S(x)\vee I(x))\rightarrow\exists y . (C(y,x)\wedge F(y))\bigr)$

# Tutorial

## Start with the domain and vocabulary

A first-order translation only has meaning after the domain and the nonlogical symbols have been defined. Check the order of the arguments in every predicate. If $C(x,y)$ means “$x$ is a close contact of $y$,” then a close contact of $x$ is described by $C(y,x)$.

## Restricted universal statements

“Every $A$ is a $B$” becomes

$$
\forall x . \bigl(A(x)\rightarrow B(x)\bigr).
$$

It does not become $\forall x . (A(x)\wedge B(x))$, which would say that everything in the domain is both an $A$ and a $B$.

## Restricted existential statements

“Some $A$ is a $B$” becomes

$$
\exists x . \bigl(A(x)\wedge B(x)\bigr).
$$

Using an implication here would be too weak: if $A(x)$ were False, then $A(x)\rightarrow B(x)$ would be True without providing an $A$ at all.

## Quantifier order

Let $K(x,y)$ mean “$x$ knows $y$.” “Everyone knows someone” becomes $\forall x . \exists y . K(x,y)$; different people may know different witnesses. “There is someone whom everyone knows” becomes $\exists y . \forall x . K(x,y)$; the same person must be known by everyone. These formulas differ only in quantifier order.

“Someone knows everyone” instead becomes $\exists y . \forall x . K(y,x)$. Here the argument order also changes: the one person is doing the knowing.

## Make scope explicit

Write a dot after each quantified variable. Its scope extends to the end of the formula unless a closing parenthesis enclosing the quantifier ends it sooner. For example, $(\forall x . V(x))\rightarrow(\forall y . \neg S(y))$ keeps the two scopes separate. Use parentheses to clarify mixed connectives as needed. For example,

$$
\forall x . \bigl(S(x)\rightarrow \exists y . (C(y,x)\wedge S(y))\bigr)
$$

says that each person with COVID has at least one close contact with COVID. The existential witness may depend on $x$.

When English is genuinely ambiguous, state the interpretation you are formalizing.

## Constants in translations

Constants name particular objects. If $a$ and $b$ name people, then

$$
S(a)\wedge R(a,b)\wedge\neg V(b)\wedge F(b)
$$

says that person $a$ has COVID and is a roommate of person $b$, while $b$ is unvaccinated and has a fever.

# Answers

## Practice Exam Problem 1

1. $\forall x . \bigl(S(x)\rightarrow \exists y . (C(y,x)\wedge V(y)\wedge\neg I(y))\bigr)$
2. $\forall x . \bigl(((\exists y . (R(x,y)\wedge S(y)))\wedge\neg S(x))\rightarrow V(x)\bigr)$
3. There is a person of whom everyone is a close contact.
4. Anyone who is a close contact of an unvaccinated person has a fever or influenza, or both.

## Practice Exam Problem 2

1. $(\forall x . V(x))\rightarrow(\forall y . \neg S(y))$
2. $\forall x . \bigl(S(x)\rightarrow\forall y . ((C(y,x)\wedge\neg V(y))\rightarrow S(y))\bigr)$
3. There is a vaccinated person none of whose roommates has influenza. The statement does not require that person to have any roommates.
4. Everyone who has COVID or influenza, or both, has a close contact with a fever.
