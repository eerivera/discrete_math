# Skill Description

The ability to translate between English statements and first-order logic in a specified language and domain. Translations must preserve quantifier order, quantifier scope, and the direction of every predicate.

---

# Sample Problems

Let the domain be a group of people, with:

* $S(x)$: $x$ has COVID;
* $F(x)$: $x$ has a fever;
* $C(x,y)$: $x$ is a close contact of $y$;
* $R(x,y)$: $x$ and $y$ are roommates;
* $V(x)$: $x$ is vaccinated; and
* $I(x)$: $x$ has influenza.

Translate Problems 1–4 into first-order logic.

1. Everyone who has COVID has a close contact who has COVID.
2. Anyone who has a roommate with COVID and does not have COVID is vaccinated.
3. If everyone is vaccinated, then no one has COVID.
4. If someone has COVID, then every unvaccinated close contact of that person has COVID.

Translate Problems 5–7 into clear English. Here $a$ and $b$ are constant symbols naming particular people.

5. $S(a)\wedge R(a,b)\wedge\neg V(b)\wedge F(b)$
6. $\exists y\,\forall x\,C(x,y)$
7. $\forall x\,\forall y\bigl(C(x,y)\rightarrow C(y,x)\bigr)$

---

# Tutorial

## Start with the domain and vocabulary

A first-order translation only has meaning after the domain and the nonlogical symbols have been defined. Check the order of the arguments in every predicate. If $C(x,y)$ means “$x$ is a close contact of $y$,” then a close contact of $x$ is described by $C(y,x)$.

## Restricted universal statements

“Every $A$ is a $B$” becomes

$$
\forall x\bigl(A(x)\rightarrow B(x)\bigr).
$$

It does not become $\forall x(A(x)\wedge B(x))$, which would say that everything in the domain is both an $A$ and a $B$.

## Restricted existential statements

“Some $A$ is a $B$” becomes

$$
\exists x\bigl(A(x)\wedge B(x)\bigr).
$$

Using an implication here would be too weak: if $A(x)$ were False, then $A(x)\rightarrow B(x)$ would be True without providing an $A$ at all.

## Quantifier order

Let $K(x,y)$ mean “$x$ knows $y$.” “Everyone knows someone” becomes $\forall x\,\exists y\,K(x,y)$; different people may know different witnesses. “There is someone whom everyone knows” becomes $\exists y\,\forall x\,K(x,y)$; the same person must be known by everyone. These formulas differ only in quantifier order.

“Someone knows everyone” instead becomes $\exists y\,\forall x\,K(y,x)$. Here the argument order also changes: the one person is doing the knowing.

## Make scope explicit

Use parentheses around the body of a quantifier when it contains mixed connectives. For example,

$$
\forall x\bigl(S(x)\rightarrow \exists y(C(y,x)\wedge S(y))\bigr)
$$

says that each person with COVID has at least one close contact with COVID. The existential witness may depend on $x$.

When English is genuinely ambiguous, state the interpretation you are formalizing.

---

# Answers

1. $\forall x\bigl(S(x)\rightarrow \exists y(C(y,x)\wedge S(y))\bigr)$
2. $\forall x\bigl(((\exists y(R(x,y)\wedge S(y)))\wedge\neg S(x))\rightarrow V(x)\bigr)$
3. $(\forall x\,V(x))\rightarrow(\forall y\,\neg S(y))$
4. $\forall x\bigl(S(x)\rightarrow\forall y((C(y,x)\wedge\neg V(y))\rightarrow S(y))\bigr)$
5. Person $a$ has COVID and is a roommate of person $b$, while person $b$ is unvaccinated and has a fever.
6. There is a person of whom everyone is a close contact.
7. Being a close contact is symmetric: whenever one person is a close contact of another, the second is also a close contact of the first.
