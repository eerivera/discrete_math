# Skill Description

Ability to compute and reason about compositions of functions with numbers, tuples, and sets as inputs or outputs. Track domains and codomains, derive formulas, and calculate images and inverse images under compositions.

---

# Sample Problems

Apply the innermost function first and show intermediate values. Here $\mathbb N=\lbrace 0,1,2,\ldots\rbrace$, $\mathbb N^+=\lbrace 1,2,3,\ldots\rbrace$, and $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)$ is the set of finite subsets of $\mathbb N$. All sums and products below are over finite sets.

## Practice Exam Problem 1

Let $D:\mathbb N^+\rightarrow\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)$ send $n$ to its positive divisors. Let $s:\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)\rightarrow\mathbb N$ be $s(S)=\sum_{x\in S}(x+1)$, and put $d=s\circ D$.

1. Find $D(14)$.
2. Find $s(\lbrace 2,5,8\rbrace)$.
3. Find $d(10)$.
4. Find and justify a formula for $d(pq)$ when $p$ and $q$ are distinct primes.

## Practice Exam Problem 2

Let $g:\mathbb Z^3\rightarrow\mathbb Z^3$ be $g(a,b,c)=(b,c,a+c)$, let $h:\mathbb Z^3\rightarrow\mathbb Z$ be $h(a,b,c)=b^2-ac$, and put $d=h\circ g$.

1. Find $g(2,4,7)$.
2. Find $h(2,4,7)$.
3. Find $d(1,3,5)$.
4. Derive a formula for $d(x,y,z)$.

## Practice Exam Problem 3

Let $g:\mathbb N^3\rightarrow\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)$ be the function that collects the distinct entries of a triple into a set. Let $h(S)=\sum_{x\in S}x$ on finite subsets of $\mathbb N$, and put $d=h\circ g$.

1. Find $g(4,2,4)$.
2. Find $h(\lbrace 1,4,7\rbrace)$.
3. Find $d(6,2,6)$.
4. Find a formula for $d(x,x+2,x)$ for $x\in\mathbb N$.

## Practice Exam Problem 4

Let $D$ be the positive-divisor function from Problem 1, and let $w:\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)\rightarrow\mathbb N$ be $w(S)=\sum_{x\in S}x^3$. Put $t=w\circ D$.

1. Find $w(\lbrace 1,3,4\rbrace)$.
2. Find $D(18)$.
3. Find $t(14)$.
4. Prove that $t(pq)=t(p)t(q)$ when $p$ and $q$ are distinct primes.

## Practice Exam Problem 5

Let $g:\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)\rightarrow\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)$ select the elements divisible by 3. Let $h:\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)\rightarrow\mathbb N$ be $h(S)=\prod_{x\in S}x$, with $h(\emptyset)=1$. Put $f=h\circ g$.

1. Find $g(\lbrace 1,3,4,6,8,9\rbrace)$.
2. Find $h(\lbrace 2,3,7\rbrace)$.
3. Find $f(\lbrace 1,2,3,4,5,6,7\rbrace)$.
4. For $n\geq1$, find and justify a formula for $f(\lbrace 1,2,\ldots,3n\rbrace)$.

## Further Practice

1. Let $u:\mathbb R\rightarrow\mathbb R$ be $u(x)=x+2$ and $v:\mathbb R\rightarrow\mathbb R$ be $v(x)=x^2$. Find $v\circ u$ and $u\circ v$. For $S=\lbrace -3,-2,0\rbrace$, find $(v\circ u)(S)$. Find $(v\circ u)^{-1}(\lbrace 1,4\rbrace)$.
2. Let $r:\mathbb R\rightarrow\mathbb R$ be the partial function $r(x)=\sqrt{x}$, defined exactly when $x\geq0$, and let $u:\mathbb R\rightarrow\mathbb R$ be $u(x)=x-5$. Where are $r\circ u$ and $u\circ r$ defined?

# Tutorial

## Composition and input types

If $g:A\rightarrow B$ and $f:B\rightarrow C$ are total functions, then their **composition** is the total function $f\circ g:A\rightarrow C$ defined by

$$
(f\circ g)(a)=f(g(a)).
$$

Read $f\circ g$ from right to left: apply $g$ first, then $f$. Record the intermediate type:

$$
a\in A\quad\longmapsto\quad g(a)\in B\quad\longmapsto\quad f(g(a))\in C.
$$

The output of the inner function must be an allowed input to the outer function. Matching codomain and domain guarantees this; more generally it suffices that the inner function's range lies in the outer function's domain. A tuple is supplied as a whole tuple, and a set as a whole set.

For example, $g:\mathbb R\rightarrow\mathbb R^2$ given by $g(x)=(x+1,2x)$ can be followed by $f:\mathbb R^2\rightarrow\mathbb R$ given by $f(a,b)=a-b$. Then $(f\circ g)(x)=(x+1)-2x=1-x$.

For partial functions, $f\circ g$ is defined at $a$ exactly when $g(a)$ is defined and $f$ is defined at $g(a)$. An intermediate undefined value stops the calculation.

## Order and grouping

In general $f\circ g$ and $g\circ f$ are different, and one order may not even have the required types. Composition is **associative** whenever the types match:

$$
(f\circ g)\circ h=f\circ(g\circ h),
$$

because both sides send $x$ to $f(g(h(x)))$. The identity function $\operatorname{id}_A(a)=a$ leaves values unchanged, so $f\circ\operatorname{id}_A=f$ and $\operatorname{id}_B\circ f=f$ for $f:A\rightarrow B$.

## Substituting into tuple formulas

If $g(a,b,c)=(a+c,b,a)$ and $h(r,s,t)=r-st$, then

$$
(h\circ g)(a,b,c)=h(a+c,b,a)=a+c-ab.
$$

Use different parameter names or write the intermediate tuple to avoid substituting an original coordinate where a new coordinate belongs. Here $h$ should have integer or real codomain, rather than $\mathbb N$, since its values can be negative.

## Set outputs and finite aggregation

When a function converts a tuple to a set, equal coordinates contribute only one element. For example, collecting $(2,5,2)$ gives $\lbrace 2,5\rbrace$. A subsequent sum gives $2+5=7$. It does not sum the original three positions.

Sets in both questions and answers must list each member exactly once. Repeated entries are allowed in tuples, but do not give valid set listings under the course convention.

A sum or product of all members of a set is defined here only for finite sets. This is why the problems use $\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)$ rather than the entire power set. We take the empty sum to be 0 and the empty product to be 1.

The positive divisors of a positive integer form a finite set. Restrict divisor inputs to $\mathbb N^+$: every positive integer divides 0, so summing all positive divisors of 0 would not be a finite sum. For distinct primes $p,q$, the positive divisors of $pq$ are $1,p,q,pq$. List the possible divisors before summing or multiplying their contributions.

## Images and inverse images of compositions

For total $g:A\rightarrow B$, $f:B\rightarrow C$, $S\subseteq A$, and $T\subseteq C$,

$$
(f\circ g)(S)=f(g(S)),\qquad
(f\circ g)^{-1}(T)=g^{-1}(f^{-1}(T)).
$$

Images follow the forward order: first send $S$ through $g$, then send its image through $f$. Inverse images work backward: first find all inputs to $f$ that reach $T$, then find all inputs to $g$ that reach that set. These are inverse images of sets; neither function needs to be bijective. The formulas also hold for partial functions when images and inverse images use only defined values.

**Proof.** An output belongs to $(f\circ g)(S)$ exactly when it is $f(g(s))$ for some $s\in S$. These are exactly the outputs in $f(g(S))$. For the second equality, an input $a\in A$ belongs to the left side exactly when $f(g(a))\in T$, equivalently $g(a)\in f^{-1}(T)$, equivalently $a\in g^{-1}(f^{-1}(T))$. Thus both equalities follow from membership in the respective sets. **Q.E.D.**

# Answers

## Practice Exam Problem 1

1. $D(14)=\lbrace 1,2,7,14\rbrace$.
2. $s(\lbrace 2,5,8\rbrace)=(2+1)+(5+1)+(8+1)=18$.
3. $d(10)=s(D(10))=s(\lbrace 1,2,5,10\rbrace)=2+3+6+11=22$.
4. For distinct primes, the positive divisors of $pq$ are exactly $1,p,q,pq$. Each contributes its value plus 1, so

$$
d(pq)=(1+1)+(p+1)+(q+1)+(pq+1)=pq+p+q+5.
$$

## Practice Exam Problem 2

1. $g(2,4,7)=(4,7,9)$.
2. $h(2,4,7)=4^2-2\cdot7=2$.
3. $d(1,3,5)=h(3,5,6)=5^2-3\cdot6=7$.
4. Substituting the whole output tuple gives

$$
d(x,y,z)=h(y,z,x+z)=z^2-y(x+z)=z^2-xy-yz.
$$

## Practice Exam Problem 3

1. $g(4,2,4)=\lbrace 2,4\rbrace$.
2. $h(\lbrace 1,4,7\rbrace)=1+4+7=12$.
3. $d(6,2,6)=h(\lbrace 2,6\rbrace)=8$.
4. Since $x$ and $x+2$ are distinct, $d(x,x+2,x)=h(\lbrace x,x+2\rbrace)=2x+2$.

## Practice Exam Problem 4

1. $w(\lbrace 1,3,4\rbrace)=1+27+64=92$.
2. $D(18)=\lbrace 1,2,3,6,9,18\rbrace$.
3. $t(14)=w(\lbrace 1,2,7,14\rbrace)=1+8+343+2744=3096$.
4. The positive divisors of $pq$ are exactly $1,p,q,pq$. Therefore

$$
t(pq)=1+p^3+q^3+p^3q^3=(1+p^3)(1+q^3)=t(p)t(q).
$$

The final equality uses the divisor sets $\lbrace 1,p\rbrace$ and $\lbrace 1,q\rbrace$ for the primes themselves. **Q.E.D.**

## Practice Exam Problem 5

1. $g(\lbrace 1,3,4,6,8,9\rbrace)=\lbrace 3,6,9\rbrace$.
2. $h(\lbrace 2,3,7\rbrace)=2\cdot3\cdot7=42$.
3. Filtering first gives $f(\lbrace 1,2,3,4,5,6,7\rbrace)=h(\lbrace 3,6\rbrace)=18$.
4. The selected elements are $3,6,\ldots,3n$. Factoring one 3 from each of the $n$ factors gives

$$
f(\lbrace 1,2,\ldots,3n\rbrace)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(3k)=3^n\prod_{k=1}^{n}k=3^n n!.
$$

## Further Practice

1. $(v\circ u)(x)=(x+2)^2$, while $(u\circ v)(x)=x^2+2$. The image of $S$ under $v\circ u$ is $\lbrace 0,1,4\rbrace$. For the inverse image, $(x+2)^2=1$ gives $x=-3,-1$ and $(x+2)^2=4$ gives $x=-4,0$, so the answer is $\lbrace -4,-3,-1,0\rbrace$. Equivalently, $v^{-1}(\lbrace 1,4\rbrace)=\lbrace -2,-1,1,2\rbrace$ and then take the inverse image under $u$.
2. $(r\circ u)(x)=\sqrt{x-5}$ is defined exactly when $x\geq5$. The other order, $(u\circ r)(x)=\sqrt{x}-5$, is defined exactly when $x\geq0$.
